How Studio extends the practice

Studio organizes eight workplace scenarios around five stages: Discover, Design, Build, Break, and Explain. Learners investigate a need, propose a workflow, examine failures, and explain their choices to a specific audience.

Explorer provides guided practice. Builder reduces the scaffolding. Architect asks learners to make and defend more of the decisions independently. All eight games are independently accessible.

Progress saves in your browser, with downloadable backups. Reporting Rescue provides a rule-based practice score; Games 2–8 ask you to review your written work against an evidence checklist.

What I would measure in a workplace pilot

I would establish a baseline, then evaluate work quality, time required, and whether participants can apply the checks independently. Completion alone would not establish proficiency.

Before collecting learner results, I would define consent, access, retention, and how the findings would guide follow-up training. Sample dashboards would be identified as illustrative.

How I lead the work

I created Applied AI Lab to turn responsible AI concepts into decisions people can practice. I set the learning goals, sequenced the two levels, and directed the work from course design through testing and publication. AI tools assisted development; I reviewed the content and behavior.

My priorities were practical exercises, clear technical explanations, independent access to each activity, and recovery of saved work. Learner testing informed revisions to the navigation, instructional support, and progress saving.

For a workplace pilot, I would establish a baseline and evaluate work quality, total human effort, and whether people can apply the checks independently. Those decisions draw on my experience teaching, leading programs, and evaluating AI work.

This is an independent prototype. Its sample analytics use synthetic data; they do not represent a completed workplace pilot.
