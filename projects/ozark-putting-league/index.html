Ozark Putting League

An app that connects player check-in, score entry, and standings for singles, doubles, and disc golf baseball. This case study shows the workflows and the actual app interface.

Overview

The app supports the steps of a putting event: check players in, assign scoring groups, record results, review corrections, and display standings. Each game has its own scoring rules and interface.

The need

League scoring needs to account for different rules, multiple players sharing a phone, and corrections before results are final. Organizers also need a way to show the current standings to the room.

I designed the app around those event tasks, with separate scoring flows for singles, doubles, and baseball.

My role

I designed and built the check-in screens, scorecards, and standings. I implemented scoring rules for all three games and tested score entry, corrections, and results.

I used AI tools to help write code, then reviewed and tested it. The public project page shows the app with sample data and explains how the games work.

Approach

  1. Follow the event sequence. Connect check-in and scoring groups to the scorecard and standings.
  2. Keep rules visible in the workflow. Use different score-entry controls for singles station scores, paired doubles putts, and baseball plays.
  3. Include review steps. Let scorekeepers review a round or confirm a turn before saving; keep organizer review for corrections and final payouts.
  4. Separate personal and shared views. Provide phone scorecards and LeagueTV displays appropriate to each format.

Implementation

Singles supports a scorekeeper recording for a group, followed by round review. Doubles records paired putts and confirms the turn. Baseball follows at-bats, runners, and game events.

The interactive screenshot gallery pairs a phone slideshow with a television display. Its game selector switches both views together. The guide explains check-in, scoring, standings, and organizer review.

Skills demonstrated

  • Operational planning: connected event setup, participation, scoring, and results in a repeatable sequence.
  • Process and interface design: translated distinct game rules into practical controls and review steps.
  • Testing and problem-solving: checked score entry, corrections, and results across three formats.
  • Documentation: created a public guide explaining the rules and event workflow.

Results & limits

The project demonstrates implemented check-in, scoring, and standings workflows for three games, including mobile and television views. The public showcase lets visitors inspect the interface and rules.

The public release contains screenshots and documentation. It makes no claim of measured time savings or completed league-wide adoption. Player records, organizer tools, and production endpoints are not published here.
